There is no split button. Video and GIF used to carry a 34px chevron pane on the right, lighter than the card at #323232, divided off with a #444444 hairline, and it opened an FPS menu. The pane rendered and opened nothing for as long as it existed, so it came off — the four cards are now one shape, whole and identical. scripts/verify-design.ps1 scans the Video card's row for that hairline and fails if a #444444 pixel comes back, so it cannot quietly return.
The frame rates moved to the header. They are fixed — 60 for video, 15 for GIF, both read from Settings — so stating them is all the panel ever needed to do. One badge carries both, written 60 / 15 FPS. Two badges do not fit: the gap to the countdown track measures 60px and a second badge needs 66px with its own gap.

Three kinds of thing, three treatments. Audio is a toggle and folder is an action, so both are chips — raised: 1px #333333 border on all four sides, 36px tall, hover state. Status is a readout, so it is recessed: same #1C1C1C fill, but no border, no top highlight and an inset 0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,.55) inner shadow, 28px tall, no hover. Same material, opposite depth — one sits on the panel, the other is cut into it.
An earlier pass left the status as bare text on the panel background. It read as a leftover rather than a field, and the recording state had nothing to anchor it. The well gives it a container without ever making it look pressable — the depth cue does the work a border would have done wrong.
Chips are content-sized and cap at 152px, truncating the device name. When the status goes live the chips shed their labels before the status truncates — a running timer outranks a folder name. Chip and status never share an edge; the 6px gap and the flex:1 spacer keep them apart at any string length.
