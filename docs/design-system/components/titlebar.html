Titlebar

30px tall. Identity on the left, two window controls on the right, and every pixel that is not a control is drag surface. There are no app actions in the bar — the panel body carries all of those.

The numbers are cumcord's, taken off its source rather than measured off its pixels: a 30px bar, 34 × 30 window controls, 13px glyphs, a 9px leading inset, and 8px between everything. One gap value for the whole bar, so the space between the mark and the wordmark is the same 8px as the space between minimize and close. Matching a native bar by eye is what produced the 22px strip this replaces.

Left cluster is the drag handle and carries identity — a 13px mark and a 12px title, both at #8A8A8A. Everything from the wordmark to the first button is grab zone, so the window is easy to move without hunting for empty pixels.

Hit boxes shown. These two are the only targets in the app that sit under the 36px floor — a titlebar control is wider than it is tall by convention. Each one runs the full height of the bar, so there is no dead strip above or below it and close still reaches the window corner.

The close button owns the corner. No trailing padding on the control cluster and no radius on the button, so its hover fill runs into the top-right corner of the window instead of leaving a miss strip beside it. The panel asks DWM for DWMWCP_DONOTROUND, so the corner really is square and the fill really does reach it — a rounded window would cut a notch out of that same fill.
Nothing in the bar holds state. Both controls are momentary. Any toggle — keep on top, a capture mode, a format — belongs in the panel body, where there is room to label it. A 34 × 30 square with a tint on it is not something a state can be read off.
Implementation on Windows. The window is opened with appears_transparent: true rather than titlebar: None. Both hide the system bar on paper, but on Windows the latter produces a window that never becomes visible. The strip does not answer WM_NCHITTEST with HTCAPTION either: that hands the drag to DefWindowProc's modal move loop, and something in this window cancels it — measured, the panel tracked the cursor the whole way and then snapped back to its starting rect the instant the button came up. GPUI's own Window::start_window_move is implemented for Wayland and X11 only. So mouse-down on the strip records the cursor's offset inside the window, and a move listener calls SetWindowPos to keep the panel under that same point until the button comes up. That listener has to be window-level: a strip-local one stops hearing moves the moment the pointer outruns the strip, which is the drag catching partway through a gesture.
